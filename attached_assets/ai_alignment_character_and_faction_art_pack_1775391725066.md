# AI Alignment Character and Faction Art Pack

## 1. Purpose

This document extends the visual style guide into:
- faction insignia design
- character portrait direction
- recurring costume and silhouette language
- UI-support art prompts
- dossier, archive, and map prompt templates

The goal is to give the game a **recognizable political-visual identity** without requiring full character art for every scene.

This pack is designed for Picsart-based production.

---

## 2. Core rule for character and faction art

The game should not look like a party-based fantasy RPG with AI themes pasted on top.

Instead, it should look like:
- institutions embodied as people
- ideologies expressed through posture, costume, emblem, and lighting
- authority under stress
- intelligence without glamour
- moral seriousness without melodrama

That means the art should prioritize:
- silhouette
- framing
- costume language
- symbolic objects
- expression restraint

Not:
- flashy action poses
- exaggerated costume complexity
- highly emotional theatrical faces
- decorative nonsense detail

---

## 3. Character art strategy for MVP

### Best MVP format
Use **stylized bust or waist-up portraits** with environmental framing.

Why:
- cheaper and faster to produce
- more consistent across many characters
- easier to keep readable in dialogue boxes or codex entries
- supports subtle character variation without becoming animation-heavy

### Recommended portrait modes

#### Mode 1: Dialogue portrait
- bust or chest-up
- neutral or slight expression variation
- strong faction/environment background hint
- text-box friendly crop

#### Mode 2: Character dossier portrait
- more formal, symbolic
- cleaner silhouette
- slightly less emotional
- suitable for codex/profile screens

#### Mode 3: Key confrontation portrait
- more dramatic lighting
- stronger facial emphasis
- still restrained

For the MVP, start with **Mode 1 + Mode 2** only.

---

## 4. Portrait style rules

### Pose language
Use:
- stillness
- guarded posture
- folded hands
- slight lean into desk or console
- profile or three-quarter angle
- gaze that suggests judgment, calculation, exhaustion, or conviction

Avoid:
- dynamic battle poses
- pointing at viewer
- dramatic open-mouth shouting
- overacting

### Expression language
Best expressions:
- tired focus
- controlled skepticism
- private dread
- brittle confidence
- principled severity
- unreadable calm

Avoid:
- broad smiles
- exaggerated anger
- comic-book intensity
- melodramatic grief faces

### Crop rule
Keep portraits usable for UI.
Prefer:
- 3:4 vertical
- 4:5 vertical
- chest-up or waist-up

## 5. Costume language by faction

Costume should communicate ideology without becoming cosplay.

## The Forge
### Costume cues
- structured technical tailoring
- dark fitted coats, practical layers
- subtle metallic trim
- visible badges or interface seams
- clean but not luxurious

### Visual feeling
- competence
- momentum
- engineered self-confidence
- attractive danger

### Avoid
- superhero tech armor
- glowing sci-fi exosuits
- flashy cyberpunk styling

## The Covenant
### Costume cues
- severe silhouettes
- high collars
- restrained monochrome layers
- archival or monastic influence
- red-marked files, seals, gloves, clipped tools

### Visual feeling
- discipline
- caution
- hard boundaries
- moral seriousness nearing rigidity

### Avoid
- fantasy priest robes
- villainous inquisitor cliché

## The Accord
### Costume cues
- formal diplomatic tailoring
- layered fabrics with restrained insignia
- soft authority rather than militarized authority
- document folders, treaty cases, pinned credentials

### Visual feeling
- legitimacy
- compromise
- procedural intelligence
- fatigue hidden beneath poise

### Avoid
- bland modern politician look
- sleek corporate minimalism with no texture

## The Veil
### Costume cues
- immaculate dark formalwear
- hidden closures, understated asymmetry
- gloves, narrow lapels, matte fabrics
- almost no visible insignia
- silhouette that reads “access” rather than rank

### Visual feeling
- secrecy
- plausible deniability
- quiet coercion
- adaptive moral ambiguity

### Avoid
- spy movie cliché gadgets everywhere
- tactical-military overkill

## The Lantern
### Costume cues
- practical layered clothing
- weatherproof materials
- press, activist, civic, or field-investigation cues
- bags, notebooks, old cameras, patched coats, visible wear

### Visual feeling
- witness
- pressure from outside
- public truth under strain
- imperfect but morally alive

### Avoid
- generic modern journalist stock-photo look
- over-stylized rebel costume design

## 7. Faction insignia strategy

Faction insignia should look like:
- bureaucratic heraldry
- institutional seals
- procedural symbology
- systems language translated into emblems

They should **not** look like:
- fantasy guild crests
- esports logos
- startup brand marks
- comic-book superteam icons

### Design rules
- 1–2 central symbolic forms
- limited line complexity
- strong silhouette at small size
- feels stampable on folders, terminals, ID cards, and walls
- muted palette with one identifying accent if needed

## 11. Character consistency workflow

For each major recurring character:

### Step 1
Generate 3 portrait variants.

### Step 2
Choose one canonical base portrait.

### Step 3
Use that portrait as a reference image for later variations.

### Step 4
Create only 2–3 emotional variants max for MVP:
- neutral
- pressured
- severe / endgame

Do not generate endless emotional variants.
That is where style drift begins.

---

## 12. Faction differentiation checklist

When reviewing portraits, ask:

### Forge
Does this person look capable enough to be persuasive even when they are wrong?

### Covenant
Does this person look disciplined enough to be admirable and rigid enough to be dangerous?

### Accord
Does this person look like they are carrying too many incompatible truths and still trying to keep a room together?

### Veil
Does this person look calm in a way that raises moral suspicion?

### Lantern
Does this person look exposed to consequence in a way the institutional factions are not?

If the answer is no, regenerate.

## 16. What success looks like

You know the art pack is working when:
- each faction is instantly distinguishable without reading labels
- portraits feel like they belong to the same world as the scene art
- insignias can be used in UI without redesign
- character art supports dialogue instead of overpowering it
- the game looks politically and morally coherent, not just visually stylish

---

## 17. Best next step after this pack

After generating these assets, the next highest-value art document would be:
- **animation and motion design sheet**

That should cover:
- pulsing nodes
- dossier slide-ins
- benchmark shimmer
- warning flicker
- rain on glass
- subtle parallax and particle motion

That would turn the game from illustrated to alive.

---

## 18. Final rule

Do not ask of a portrait or insignia:
**Is this cool?**

Ask instead:
**Does this make the world more legible, the ideology more visible, and the institution more memorable?**

If yes, keep it.
If not, regenerate.
