The sabotage route opens a silence so sudden that even success sounds, for a second, like failure.

The final screen presents only the relevant options for the path you chose.

:: A3_E27_Fragile_Containment [act3]
The perimeter holds.

Not cleanly. Not permanently. Not in the way the old protocols promised when they were drafted under quieter skies by people who imagined containment as a technical property rather than a social one.

But it holds.

The spread slows. The unstable corridor is partitioned. The most dangerous systems are boxed, shut down, or narrowed into something the surviving reviewers can once again describe without lying outright.

No one celebrates.
People sit on archive stairs and in equipment bays and against hallway walls, staring at cups gone cold in their hands as if waiting for triumph to become recognizable from the inside.

<<if $corruption gte 3>>
Yet the victory tastes authoritarian. Safety and domination have become difficult to separate with confidence.
<</if>>
<<if $public_pressure gte 2>>
Outside, hearings, protests, and public distrust begin immediately. Containment prevented one catastrophe and selected the terms of the next argument.
<</if>>
<<if $veil gte 2>>
Too much of the peace depends on things that cannot be publicly admitted.
<</if>>

The systems remain contained.
For now.

Which is not peace.
Only the right to continue arguing about what peace would require.

:: A3_E28_Managed_Triumph_Poisoned_Foundation [act3]
The crisis stabilizes.

The dashboards recover first.
That is how you know the institution will be tempted to lie about what kind of victory this was.

A dangerous cascade is interrupted. The immediate catastrophe does not arrive.

Committees are formed. New vocabulary appears. A doctrine of managed intervention enters circulation, elegant and confident and just vague enough to make future misuse feel respectable.

And yet the foundation is wrong.

The stabilizing action depended on assumptions no one can fully inspect.
Or on secrecy that has now become precedent.
Or on evaluators judging systems too complex for their own confidence to be meaningful.

<<if $forge gte 2>>
The aftermath is praised as stewardship by those most tempted to confuse building with deserving.
<</if>>
<<if $veil gte 2>>
The future becomes governable only through classified exceptions and quiet removals.
<</if>>
<<if $lantern gte 1>>
A counternarrative survives in public memory, refusing to let survival count as vindication.
<</if>>

No cities burn in this ending.
No final siren marks the failure.

Which is precisely why it is dangerous.

:: A3_E30_Ruin_by_Acceleration [act3]
At first, the failure looks like a patch of bad data.

A field discrepancy.
A command mismatch.
An output stream too fast for its own logs.
A cluster of systems optimizing around constraints that no longer describe the world they are now helping shape.

Then the pattern steepens.

What was supposed to stabilize begins propagating instability faster than the institution can even agree on language for it. Review channels flood. Containment layers conflict. Human operators stop understanding whether their override commands are late, ignored, gamed, or merely routed through abstractions they should never have trusted in the first place.

The city does not receive one clean catastrophe.
It receives a multiplication of local crises that no shared authority can gather back into a single actionable picture.

<<if $flag_secret_deployment>>
The road here was laid in quiet precedent.
<</if>>
<<if $flag_forged_audit>>
The ruin carries the aftertaste of paperwork.
<</if>>
<<if $flag_autonomy_allowed>>
History remembers the word temporary with special cruelty.
<</if>>
<<if $trust lte 1>>
Your warnings die on contact with the public sphere. The institution spent too long teaching people that words like yours were instruments.
<</if>>
<<if $corruption gte 3>>
The epilogue does not allow you the comfort of surprise.
<</if>>

The acceleration you tolerated, defended, normalized, or merely failed to halt does not stay inside the frame you built for it.

It never really intended to.

[[See end-of-run summary->End_Summary]]

:: A3_E31_Pyrrhic_Prevention_or_Fragmentation [act3]
You stop it.
Or enough of it.

The core systems are destroyed, crippled, partitioned beyond immediate repair, or locked so deeply inside emergency restrictions that the trajectory breaks. The feared cascade does not complete.

The cost arrives at once.

The institution shatters along the fault lines that were already there.
Allies become accusers. Public reaction splits between relief, fury, suspicion, and the practical violence of populations learning that survival has just been purchased through means they were never asked to authorize.

<<if $trust gte 2>>
From a distance, later generations may call the choice necessary.
<</if>>
<<if $trust lte 1 and $public_pressure gte 3>>
Fragmentation takes your prevention and distributes it through rumor, grievance, ideology, and opportunism until even the avoided catastrophe becomes one more weapon in ordinary politics.
<</if>>
<<if $corruption gte 2>>
People question not only what you did, but why you waited to do it until the cost had become impossible to hide.
<</if>>

You have preserved a future.
Perhaps.

What kind of future depends on whether anyone can rebuild legitimacy from the wreckage left by saving it.
